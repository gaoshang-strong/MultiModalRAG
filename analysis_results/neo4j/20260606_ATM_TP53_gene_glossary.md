## 🔵 ATM Kinase Cascade

### ATM (Ataxia Telangiectasia Mutated)
- **Protein type:** PI3K-like serine/threonine kinase (PIKK family)
- **Activation:** Recruited to DSBs by MRN complex; activated by KAT5-mediated acetylation at K3016; autophosphorylates at S1981 → monomer → active kinase
- **Key substrates:** H2AX(S139), MDC1, CHEK2(T68), TP53(S15), MDM2(S395), BRCA1(S1387)
- **Why important for ME:** The central kinase of DSB response; its loss abolishes the entire left arm of the DDR. Single ATM loss is tolerated because TP53 and CHEK2 maintain checkpoint function. Dual ATM+TP53 loss eliminates all checkpoint, repair signaling, and apoptotic capacity.

### MRN Complex (MRE11–RAD50–NBN)
- **MRE11:** Nuclease (3'→5' exonuclease + endonuclease); processes DSB ends; forms complex with RAD50 and NBN
- **RAD50:** ATPase; coiled-coil structure that tethers DNA ends; part of MRN sensor complex
- **NBN (NBS1/Nibrin):** Adaptor protein; contains FHA and BRCT domains; bridges MRN to ATM; phosphorylated by ATM (S343) in a positive feedback loop
- **Why important:** MRN is the initial sensor — it arrives at DSBs within seconds, recruits ATM, and activates its kinase. Loss of any MRN subunit abolishes ATM activation at DSBs. NBN is listed in Reactome as shared with TP53 pathway because TP53 can transcriptionally regulate NBN.

### MDC1 (Mediator of DNA Checkpoint 1)
- **Function:** Scaffold protein; binds γH2AX via BRCT domains; recruits RNF8 and maintains ATM at damage sites; amplifies the damage signal by spreading γH2AX along chromatin
- **Regulation:** Phosphorylated by ATM at multiple sites; also phosphorylated by DYRK2
- **Key interactions:** γH2AX → MDC1 → RNF8 (essential relay in the ubiquitin cascade)

### H2AX (γH2AX)
- **Function:** Histone H2A variant; phosphorylated at S139 (= γH2AX) by ATM within seconds of DSB formation. γH2AX spreads over megabases of chromatin flanking the break, creating a landing platform for repair factors
- **Why a biomarker:** γH2AX foci are the gold-standard marker of DSBs; their count and disappearance kinetics measure DSB induction and repair
- **Note:** H2AX is ATM-specific in Reactome (not in TP53 pathways), but ATR and DNA-PK can also phosphorylate it

### RNF8 (Ring Finger Protein 8)
- **Function:** E3 ubiquitin ligase; recruited to DSBs by MDC1; ubiquitinates histone H1 (K13/K15) and histone H2A, creating the first ubiquitin signal at the break
- **Cascade role:** Ubiquitylated H1/H2A by RNF8 → docking site for RNF168; this cascade converts a local phosphorylation signal (γH2AX) into a chromatin-wide ubiquitin landscape
- **Clinical:** RNF8 loss impairs both NHEJ and HR; associated with checkpoint defects

### RNF168 (Ring Finger Protein 168)
- **Function:** E3 ubiquitin ligase; amplifies the RNF8 signal by ubiquitinating H2A at K13/K15 and K27; creates docking sites for 53BP1 (NHEJ) and BRCA1/PALB2 (HR)
- **Regulation:** Recruited by ubiquitin marks from RNF8; its own stability regulated by HERC2 (deubiquitinase-counterbalance) and BRCC3 (deubiquitinase)
- **Pathway choice:** High RNF168 favors 53BP1/NHEJ; low RNF168 or S-phase context favors HR via BRCA1

### 53BP1 (TP53BP1 / p53-binding protein 1)
- **Function:** Large scaffold protein (~220 kDa); binds H4K20me2 and H2A-K15-Ub (generated by RNF168); recruits PTIP, RIF1, SHIELDIN complex → blocks end resection → promotes NHEJ
- **Antagonism with BRCA1:** 53BP1 promotes NHEJ; BRCA1 removes 53BP1 from break ends in S/G2 to allow end resection and HR. Their balance determines repair pathway choice.
- **Cell cycle:** Highly expressed and active in G1; displaced from breaks in S/G2 by BRCA1-CtIP complex
- **Cancer relevance:** 53BP1 loss causes NHEJ defects; in BRCA1-mutant tumors, 53BP1 loss restores HR (PARPi resistance)

### ABRAXAS1 (FAM175A)
- **Function:** Core component of BRCA1-A complex; bridges BRCA1-BRCT domain to the ubiquitin machinery at DSBs; contains ubiquitin-interacting motif (UIM) that binds K63-ubiquitin chains
- **Complex:** BRCA1–ABRAXAS1–BARD1–UIMC1–BABAM1–BABAM2–BRCC3 = "BRCA1-A complex"
- **Why ATM-specific:** Entirely within the ATM-driven DSB ubiquitin cascade; no TP53 transcriptional connection

### UIMC1 (RAP80)
- **Function:** Ubiquitin interaction motif containing protein; recognizes K63-polyubiquitin chains at DSBs; recruits BRCA1-A complex; tethers BRCA1 to damage foci
- **Mechanism:** K63-Ub chains (built by RNF8/RNF168 + UBC13/UEV1A) → RAP80 UIM → BRCA1 BRCT recruitment

### BABAM1 (MERIT40 / NBA1)
- **Function:** Component of BRCA1-A complex; stabilizes BRCA1 at DSBs; required for G2/M checkpoint activation
- **Loss phenotype:** BABAM1 knockout cells show hypersensitivity to ionizing radiation and impaired BRCA1 focus formation

### HERC2
- **Function:** Large HECT-domain E3 ubiquitin ligase; regulates RNF168 protein stability (acts as a negative regulator); also ubiquitinates XPA (NER pathway)
- **Dual role:** Limits the extent of DSB ubiquitin signaling (prevents uncontrolled spreading); mutations in HERC2 cause intellectual disability

### BRCC3 (KIAA0157)
- **Function:** Zinc-dependent metalloprotease; K63-specific deubiquitinase (DUB); part of both BRCA1-A complex and cytoplasmic BRISC complex; removes K63-Ub from H2A, counterbalancing RNF8/RNF168
- **Regulatory role:** Fine-tunes the amplitude of the ubiquitin signal at breaks; ensures proper 53BP1/BRCA1 balance

## 🔴 TP53 Transcriptional Programme

### TP53 (Tumor Protein p53)
- **Protein type:** Sequence-specific transcription factor; tetramer; C-terminal regulatory domain, central DNA-binding domain, N-terminal transactivation domain
- **Activation:** Stabilized by: ATM phosphorylation (S15, S20), CHEK2 phosphorylation (S20), KAT5 acetylation (K382), DYRK2 phosphorylation (S46). MDM2/MDM4 normally target it for proteasomal degradation.
- **Outputs:** Transcribes >500 target genes: cell cycle arrest (CDKN1A), apoptosis (BAX, PUMA, NOXA, BID), senescence (CDKN2A), DNA repair (BRCA1, XPC, GADD45), metabolism (TIGAR), autophagy (DRAM1)
- **Why central to ME:** TP53 is the "executive TF" of DDR. Without TP53, ATM kinase cascade can still function but cannot mount a transcriptional response. ATM can phosphorylate CHEK2 to activate cell cycle arrest, providing partial compensation.

### MDM2 (Mouse Double Minute 2)
- **Function:** E3 ubiquitin ligase; principal negative regulator of TP53; ubiquitinates TP53 at K370/K372/K373/K381/K382/K386 → nuclear export and proteasomal degradation; also binds TP53 transactivation domain to inhibit transcription
- **TP53 feedback loop:** TP53 → transcribes MDM2 → MDM2 degrades TP53. This creates an autoregulatory oscillation after damage.
- **ATM connection:** ATM phosphorylates MDM2 at S395 → blocks MDM2-mediated TP53 nuclear export → TP53 stabilization

### MDM4 (MDMX)
- **Function:** Structural homolog of MDM2; does NOT have E3 ligase activity alone; forms MDM2-MDM4 heterodimer that more potently suppresses TP53 than either alone; blocks TP53 transactivation
- **Cancer:** Amplified in ~10-20% of cancers (especially retinoblastoma, melanoma); causes p53 inactivation without TP53 mutation itself

### CDKN1A (p21 / WAF1 / CIP1)
- **Function:** CDK inhibitor; binds and inhibits CDK2/cyclin E and CDK4/cyclin D complexes → G1/S arrest; also inhibits CDK1/cyclin B → G2/M arrest; can inhibit PCNA-mediated DNA replication
- **TP53 regulation:** The most potently and rapidly induced TP53 target; p21 promoter contains two TP53-binding sites
- **ATM-independent induction:** FOXO3 (phosphorylated by ATM→CHEK2 axis), MAPK14/p38 → MAPKAP2 → p21; these provide partial compensation after TP53 loss
- **Dual role:** Anti-apoptotic at high levels (blocks cell death by inhibiting CDK2) vs pro-arrest at moderate levels

### BAX and PUMA (BCL2 family)
- **BAX:** Pro-apoptotic BCL2 family member; forms pores in the outer mitochondrial membrane → cytochrome c release → caspase cascade; directly transcribed by TP53
- **PUMA (BBC3):** BH3-only protein; binds and inhibits all anti-apoptotic BCL2 members (BCL2, BCL-XL, MCL1); considered the most potent TP53-induced apoptosis effector; required for TP53-mediated apoptosis in many cell types
- **Together:** The "commitment point" for TP53-driven apoptosis; tumor cells often downregulate one or both

### GADD45 (GADD45A/B/G)
- **Function:** Growth Arrest and DNA Damage protein; TP53 transcriptional target; activates NF2/MEKK4 → p38/JNK pathways; inhibits CDK1/cyclin B → G2/M arrest; participates in NER by stimulating XPG activity; promotes senescence via CDKN1A
- **Multiple pathways:** Also induced by BRCA1 (independent of TP53); involved in demethylation via TDG-BER mechanism

### CCNG1 (Cyclin G1)
- **Function:** Atypical cyclin; transcribed by TP53; recruits PP2A phosphatase to MDM2 → dephosphorylates MDM2 → enhances MDM2-TP53 interaction. This creates a negative feedback loop (TP53 → CCNG1 → MDM2 → TP53↓) to dampen the response after repair.
- **Context:** Primarily a feedback dampener; can also interact with CDK5

### XPC (Xeroderma Pigmentosum Complementation Group C)
- **Function:** DNA damage recognition factor for global-genome NER (GG-NER); recognizes helix-distorting lesions (UV dimers, bulky adducts); recruits TFIIH complex to initiate NER
- **TP53 regulation:** TP53 transcriptionally upregulates XPC, linking DSB-induced TP53 activation to NER capacity
- **Clinical:** XPC mutations cause Xeroderma Pigmentosum (extreme UV sensitivity and skin cancer)

### E2F1 (E2F Transcription Factor 1)
- **Dual function:** Pro-proliferative transcription factor (drives G1→S); BUT can also activate apoptosis by inducing TP53 (via p14ARF → MDM2 inhibition) and directly transactivating apoptotic genes (APAF1, caspase-3, p73)
- **Why in convergence zone (BioGRID):** Physically interacts with both ATM (ATM-E2F1 axis in S-phase checkpoint) and TP53 (co-regulation of apoptosis genes)

## 🟣 Convergence Zone

### KAT5 (TIP60 / HTATIP)
- **Function:** MYST-family histone acetyltransferase; acetylates H4K16 and H2AK5 at DSBs (required for chromatin opening and repair); acetylates ATM at K3016 (required for ATM kinase activation); acetylates TP53 at K120 (in the DNA-binding domain, promotes apoptosis over arrest)
- **Why top convergence node:** KAT5 is a co-activator for BOTH ATM (at DSBs) and TP53 (for apoptotic target selection). It is shared in Reactome (14 shared genes) and is a top synthetic lethality target.
- **Synthetic lethality:** KAT5 knockout cells show hypersensitivity to DNA damage; KAT5 inhibitors are being tested as ATM-pathway vulnerabilities

### CHEK2 (Checkpoint Kinase 2)
- **Function:** Serine/threonine kinase; activated by ATM phosphorylation at T68; amplifies the ATM signal; phosphorylates TP53 at S20 (stabilizes TP53 by disrupting MDM2 binding); phosphorylates CDC25A/C → CDK inhibition → G1 and G2/M arrest; phosphorylates BRCA1 at S988 (HR); can induce apoptosis via PML-TP53 pathway
- **Why the #1 convergence node:** CHEK2 is the kinase that bridges ATM to TP53. When ATM is active, CHEK2 phosphorylates TP53 (along with ATM direct phosphorylation). When TP53 is mutated, CHEK2 can still trigger G2/M arrest and apoptosis through TP53-independent mechanisms.
- **Dual ME relevance:** CHEK2 loss-of-function mutations (1100delC) in familial cancer predispose to breast/colorectal cancer with incomplete penetrance — because TP53 compensates for checkpoint function

### CHEK1 (Checkpoint Kinase 1)
- **Function:** Serine/threonine kinase; primarily activated by ATR (in response to ssDNA/replication stress), but also by ATM (at DSBs in S/G2); phosphorylates CDC25A → CDK2 inhibition → intra-S checkpoint; phosphorylates CDC25C → G2/M arrest; phosphorylates and stabilizes Claspin
- **ATR-CHEK1 axis:** The "sister" checkpoint to ATM-CHEK2; while ATM-CHEK2 handles DSBs, ATR-CHEK1 handles replication stress and ssDNA. Both feed into the same cell cycle arrest effectors.
- **Why in convergence zone:** CHEK1 takes ATR-relayed signals from ATM (ATM→ATR→CHEK1 crosstalk at DSBs in S-phase) and converges on the same G2/M effectors as CHEK2

### ATR (ATM and Rad3-related)
- **Function:** PI3K-like kinase (PIKK family); activated at ssDNA (generated by resection of DSBs or stalled replication forks) via ATRIP-RPA complex; primary activator of CHEK1; phosphorylates many of the same targets as ATM (BRCA1, TP53, H2AX)
- **ATM-ATR crosstalk:** DSBs activate ATM first; DSB end resection generates ssDNA → activates ATR → additional CHEK1 activation. In this way, a single DSB can activate both kinase cascades.
- **Cancer:** ATR inhibitors (e.g., ceralasertib) are in clinical trials; most effective in cancers with replication stress

### BRCA1 (Breast Cancer 1)
- **Function:** Multifunctional scaffold; E3 ubiquitin ligase (with BARD1); forms multiple complexes: BRCA1-A (ubiquitin reading, DSB localization), BRCA1-B/C (HR), BRCA1-CtIP (end resection). Key roles: G2/M checkpoint (activated by ATM phosphorylation), promotes DSB end resection → HR, removes 53BP1 to switch from NHEJ to HR in S/G2, can transcriptionally activate genes including BRCA2 and RAD51
- **Shared ATM+TP53:** Phosphorylated by ATM (S1387) = ATM-dependent; transcriptionally induced by TP53 = TP53-dependent. This dual regulation makes BRCA1 a top convergence node.
- **Synthetic lethality paradigm:** BRCA1 loss + PARP inhibition = canonical synthetic lethality (HR defect + no SSB repair)

### RAD51
- **Function:** Recombinase; forms nucleoprotein filaments on ssDNA overhangs; catalyzes strand invasion into homologous dsDNA → template for repair of DSBs by HR; requires BRCA2 as mediator to displace RPA and load RAD51 onto ssDNA
- **Regulation:** Recruited by BRCA1/BRCA2; transcriptionally regulated by TP53 (can be repressed); activity modulated by RAD51AP1, SWSAP1
- **Cancer:** RAD51 overexpression correlates with chemoresistance; RAD51 paralogs (RAD51B/C/D, XRCC2/3) are tumor suppressors

### PCNA (Proliferating Cell Nuclear Antigen)
- **Function:** Homotrimeric sliding clamp; loaded by RFC onto primer-template junctions; processivity factor for replicative DNA polymerases (δ, ε); also required for mismatch repair, NER, and translesion synthesis (TLS) polymerases
- **At DSBs:** Ubiquitinated at K164 by RAD6/RAD18 (monoUb → TLS) or by SHPRH/HLTF (polyUb → template switching); regulated by ATM-dependent ubiquitination in damage response
- **TP53 connection:** TP53 can directly bind PCNA and inhibit replication (tumor suppressor function); TP53 also transcriptionally regulates p21 which binds PCNA to block CDK2 and replication

### FOXO3 (Forkhead Box O3)
- **Function:** Transcription factor; activates cell cycle arrest (CDKN1A, CDKN1B/p27), apoptosis (BIM, FASL), DNA damage response, and autophagy genes; normally inhibited by AKT phosphorylation → cytoplasmic retention
- **ATM connection:** ATM phosphorylates FOXO3 at S194 in response to DSBs → nuclear localization → DDR gene expression; independent of TP53
- **TP53 connection:** TP53 transcriptionally activates FOXO3; FOXO3 and TP53 share many target genes (p21, BIM)
- **Why convergence:** FOXO3 provides an ATM-driven, TP53-independent cell cycle arrest mechanism. In TP53-mutant cells, ATM→FOXO3→p21 axis can partially compensate for TP53 loss.

### DYRK2 (Dual-specificity Tyrosine Regulated Kinase 2)
- **Function:** Serine/threonine kinase; phosphorylates TP53 at S46 (specifically promotes apoptotic target gene selection, distinct from S15); also phosphorylates MDM2 to promote MDM2 auto-ubiquitination and degradation → further TP53 stabilization; substrate of ATM (ATM-DYRK2 axis)
- **Tumor suppressor:** DYRK2 deletion in cancers correlates with poor prognosis; promotes apoptosis over survival; promotes ubiquitin-mediated degradation of MYC, cyclin D1, MCL1 (via EDVP E3 complex)

### MAPK8 (JNK1)
- **Function:** Stress-activated kinase; phosphorylates TP53 at multiple sites (S34, T81) under stress; activates the mitochondrial apoptosis pathway independently of TP53 (via BIM phosphorylation, BCL2 inhibition); activated by ATM through RAC1→ASK1 axis
- **Why convergence:** ATM activates JNK (TP53-independent apoptosis); TP53 transcriptionally activates JNK pathway components. When BOTH are lost, stress-induced apoptosis is blocked from two independent directions.

### SETD2 (SET Domain Containing 2)
- **Function:** Only H3K36 trimethyltransferase in humans; H3K36me3 recruits MMR machinery (MSH6 reader) and HR factors (LEDGF); required for efficient DSB repair by HR
- **ATM connection:** ATM phosphorylates SETD2 (S2059) in response to DNA damage; H3K36me3 loss impairs HR at DSBs
- **TP53 connection:** SETD2 mutations are frequent in clear cell RCC, AML; often co-occur with TP53 pathway alterations
- **Cancer:** Tumor suppressor; ~15% mutation rate in TCGA; loss causes microsatellite instability and impaired MMR

### STK11 (LKB1)
- **Function:** Serine/threonine kinase; activates AMPK → energy sensing; activates CHEK2 in some contexts; required for p53-induced apoptosis in certain tissues (Peutz-Jeghers syndrome gene)
- **TP53 crosstalk:** LKB1/STK11 phosphorylates p53 (S15 in some reports); forms a complex with BRCA1; co-mutation with TP53 is common in lung adenocarcinoma

### BLM (Bloom Syndrome Helicase)
- **Function:** RecQ family helicase; 3'→5' helicase activity on forked DNA; promotes long-range resection at DSBs (with DNA2 and RPA); suppresses crossover during HR (maintains genome stability); part of BTRR complex (BLM-TOP3A-RMI1-RMI2)
- **HR role:** Essential for disrupting Holliday junctions; BLM-deficient cells show hyper-recombination and genome instability

### WRN (Werner Syndrome Helicase)
- **Function:** RecQ family helicase + exonuclease; preferentially unwinds unusual DNA structures (G-quadruplexes, D-loops); participates in both NHEJ and HR; required for replication fork restart; telomere maintenance
- **ATM connection:** ATM phosphorylates WRN at S1141 after DSBs; WRN co-localizes with MRN complex at DSBs
- **Synthetic lethality:** WRN is a synthetic lethal target in microsatellite instable (MSI-H) cancers — clinical trials ongoing

### MSH2 (MutS Homolog 2)
- **Function:** Core MMR protein; forms MutSα (with MSH6) for mismatch and insertion-loop recognition, or MutSβ (with MSH3) for larger loops; after mismatch binding, recruits MLH1/PMS2 for MMR
- **DSB connection:** MMR proteins activate ATR at O6-methylG damage (futile MMR cycles generate ssDNA); MSH2-MSH6 can activate ATM-CHEK2 pathway at DSBs; deficiency causes microsatellite instability (MSI-H), hypermutation
- **Why convergence zone (BioGRID):** Physical interaction with both ATM (in checkpoint signaling) and TP53 (direct protein-protein interaction, TP53-MSH2 complex influences MMR efficiency)

## 🟢 Cell Fate Outputs

### Cell Cycle Arrest
- **Mechanism:** G1/S arrest: ATM→CHEK2→TP53→CDKN1A/p21→CDK2-CyclinE inhibition; G2/M arrest: ATM/ATR→CHEK1/2→CDC25C phosphorylation → CDK1 inhibition; also 53BP1-mediated G2 arrest independently of TP53
- **Purpose:** Provides time for DNA repair before replication or division

### DNA Repair
- **Mechanisms:** NHEJ (53BP1-pathway, dominant in G1); HR (BRCA1/RAD51, dominant in S/G2); also NER (XPC, GADD45), MMR (MSH2)
- **ATM vs TP53:** ATM orchestrates repair pathway choice and chromatin modifications; TP53 transcribes repair factors (BRCA1, XPC, GADD45) but does not directly catalyze repair

### Apoptosis
- **Mechanism:** Intrinsic mitochondrial apoptosis: TP53→BAX/PUMA → outer mitochondrial membrane permeabilization → cytochrome c → apoptosome → caspase-9/3; extrinsic: CHEK2-mediated through TP53 and TP53-independent JNK pathway
- **Synthetic lethality implication:** If BOTH ATM and TP53 are lost, tumor cells cannot eliminate genomically unstable progeny via apoptosis → accumulate mutations → but also become dependent on mitotic checkpoint (WEE1, PLK1 inhibitor targets)

### Senescence
- **Mechanism:** GADD45 → p38/JNK activation → p16-Rb pathway reinforcement; CDKN1A/p21 sustained induction → stable cell cycle exit; SAHF (Senescence-Associated Heterochromatin Foci) formation
- **Oncogenesis relevance:** Early oncogene-induced senescence (OIS) is a tumor suppressor barrier; late-stage cancers often bypass senescence through TP53 or p16/CDKN2A loss
